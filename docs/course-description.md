# Course Description

## Introduction to Quantum Computing

**Course Title:** Introduction to Quantum Computing

**Target Audience:** Upper-division undergraduate students and beginning graduate students in computer science, electrical engineering, physics, or related STEM disciplines. The course is also suitable for self-directed learners with the appropriate mathematical background.

**Prerequisites:**

- Linear algebra (vector spaces, matrices, eigenvalues, eigenvectors)
- Basic probability and statistics
- Introductory physics (classical mechanics, electromagnetism)
- Familiarity with complex numbers and basic calculus
- No prior quantum mechanics knowledge required

**Course Overview:**

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to quantum computing, covering the theoretical foundations, mathematical formalism, major quantum algorithms, leading hardware platforms, and the path toward practical quantum advantage. Students will learn the principles of quantum mechanics as applied to computation, explore gate-based and annealing-based paradigms, and gain hands-on experience with quantum programming tools such as IBM Qiskit.

**Topics Covered:**

1. Classical vs. quantum information and computation
2. Qubits, superposition, and measurement
3. Mathematical formalism: Dirac notation, Hilbert spaces, unitary transformations
4. Single-qubit and multi-qubit quantum gates
5. Quantum entanglement and Bell states
6. Quantum circuits and circuit design
7. Quantum teleportation and superdense coding
8. Quantum algorithms: Deutsch-Josza, Grover's search, Shor's factoring
9. Quantum error correction and fault tolerance
10. Quantum annealing and optimization (D-Wave, Ising models, QUBO)
11. Trapped ion quantum computing (IonQ architecture)
12. Superconducting qubit platforms (IBM transmon architecture)
13. Quantum hardware roadmaps and the path to quantum advantage
14. Quantum programming with Qiskit

**Topics Excluded:**

- Advanced quantum field theory
- Topological quantum computing (beyond brief mention)
- Quantum machine learning (beyond introductory concepts)
- Detailed semiconductor fabrication processes
- Quantum networking and quantum internet protocols (beyond teleportation)

## Learning Outcomes

After completing this course, students will be able to:

### Remember

- Recall the postulates of quantum mechanics relevant to quantum computing
- List the major quantum gate operations (Pauli-X, Y, Z, Hadamard, CNOT, Toffoli) and their matrix representations
- Identify the key hardware platforms for quantum computing (superconducting, trapped ion, quantum annealing)
- Name the major quantum algorithms and their computational advantages

### Understand

- Explain the difference between classical bits and qubits, including superposition and measurement
- Describe how quantum entanglement enables protocols like teleportation and superdense coding
- Interpret Bloch sphere representations of single-qubit states
- Explain the principles behind quantum error correction codes

### Apply

- Construct quantum circuits using Dirac notation and matrix algebra
- Use IBM Qiskit to build, simulate, and run quantum circuits
- Calculate measurement probabilities for multi-qubit quantum states
- Apply quantum gates to transform qubit states and verify results

### Analyze

- Compare gate-based quantum computing with quantum annealing approaches
- Analyze the trade-offs between different quantum hardware platforms (superconducting vs. trapped ion)
- Evaluate the error rates and fidelity metrics of quantum processors
- Examine the role of c-couplers and l-couplers in scaling quantum systems

### Evaluate

- Assess the feasibility of quantum advantage claims for specific computational problems
- Evaluate IBM's quantum roadmap milestones and their technical requirements
- Judge the suitability of different quantum algorithms for given problem types
- Critique current approaches to quantum error correction and fault tolerance

### Create

- Design quantum circuits to solve specific computational problems
- Formulate optimization problems as QUBO/Ising models for quantum annealers
- Develop quantum teleportation and superdense coding protocols from first principles
- Propose approaches for mapping real-world problems to quantum algorithms

## Course Context

Quantum computing is at an inflection point. Major technology companies — IBM, Google, IonQ, and D-Wave — are racing to demonstrate quantum advantage, the point at which quantum computers solve problems beyond classical reach. This course equips students with the foundational knowledge to understand, evaluate, and contribute to this rapidly evolving field. By studying both the theory and the leading hardware platforms, students gain a uniquely well-rounded perspective on where quantum computing stands today and where it is heading.
